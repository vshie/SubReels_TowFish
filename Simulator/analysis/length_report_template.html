SubReels towfish · simulator study · 24–25 Nov 2026

How short can the towfish be?

The 50 mm twin-tube spine with an upright V tail, shortened from 1000 mm in steps, at 1×, 1.5× and 2× tail area, plus two layouts with no spine at all. Every layout had to hold a straight line, fly the hairpins without a roll past 45°, and change depth from 1 m to the tether's limit and back.

~680 mm spinewith today's tail (two 155 × 87 mm panels) is the shortest with real margin in the hairpins (worst roll 34°). That's about a third shorter than 1 m. Its one flag: 12° of pitch wander when towed at 0.8 m/s on the 25 m tether, right at the limit.
~630 mm is the edge632 mm with today's tail passed every test, but rolled 42° in the tight 25 m hairpin, 3° from failing. A 1.5× tail (two 190 × 107 mm panels) at that length flew the hairpins best of all (worst 28°).
A spine is neededTowed from a hoop on the tube with no tail, the fish rolls over in a straight line. A V on the end cap needs 3× the area just to fly straight, and it still wanders.

Without a spine

The hoop-over-the-CG runs didn't settle normally (the 8 m run sat at a fixed 2.04 m and needed auto-righting), so read them only as a failure, not a measurement. A tail right behind the end cap is only about 90 mm behind the CG, against 350–670 mm on a spine. It needs roughly as much area times arm as the spine tails, which is why 3× barely flies straight.

What else the runs showed

  • Depth changes don't depend on length. Every spine length dived and climbed at the same rate: about 0.08 m/s down and 0.12 m/s up on the 8 m tether, and 0.17 m/s down and 0.23 m/s up on the 25 m. Roll stayed under 6° in transit.
  • Holding a straight line is easy for any spine. All 15 spine layouts held within ±0.3 m and under 10° of roll. Shorter ones wobble a little more: up to 2.4° of heading wander settling down at 582 mm, against 0.1° at 1 m.
  • The one weakness of a short fish is slow towing on the long tether. At 0.8 m/s holding 8 m on the 25 m tether, pitch ranged 12–14° on the 582–682 mm layouts and 9° on the 1 m.
  • Tail area and arm trade off. The 582 mm spine failed with today's tail (a 45° roll in the tight 25 m hairpin) but passed with 1.5× area. As a rule of thumb, keep tail area × arm above about 8,500 cm³: today's 270 cm² at 32 cm or more behind the CG.
  • At the surface without auto-righting, every layout rolled. That was the depth-change test overshooting to the surface, and the extension's auto-righting covers it in service. It isn't a length effect.

Simulation: ArduSub SITL with the towed-body physics; Kevlar tether, 40/30 pilot speed caps, the shipped extension in the holds and hairpins. The tail is modelled as printed: 3 mm PETG, 3 perimeters, 10% gyroid, flooded core, about 47 g for today's V. The fish's mass is otherwise unchanged and the CG moves with the tail. The model still differs from the 4 Nov log by about 4° of straight-line pitch, so treat the boundary as approximate and confirm the short layout in the water before cutting tubes for good.
